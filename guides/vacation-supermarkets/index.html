Spanish Supermarket Chains: A Simple Guide to Supermarkets in Spain (2026)

A simple guide to Spanish supermarket chains: Mercadona, Carrefour, Lidl, Dia, Aldi, Eroski, Alcampo and Consum, plus opening hours, bags, paying and online shopping. Spain’s main national supermarket chains are Mercadona, Carrefour, Lidl, Dia, Aldi, Eroski and Alcampo. Regional chains sit alongside them, such as Consum in the Valencia region, Murcia and Catalonia. Shops may not give plastic bags away for free, so bring your own. Sunday and holiday opening depends on the region and the town. Facts below were checked on the chains’ own pages and official sources on 9 October 2026.

Supermarkets in Spain: the basics

What this is for
A plain introduction to supermarkets in Spain: the main chains and what each one is, regional chains, opening hours and Sundays, paying, bags, fresh counters and online shopping. This page does not list prices or say which chain is cheapest, because prices change all the time.
Next 3 steps
  1. Find the chains near you with the store finder on each chain’s official site (linked below). Store pages list opening hours and special holiday hours.
  2. Take a reusable bag with you. Shops in Spain may not give plastic bags away for free.
  3. On a Sunday or public holiday, check the store’s page before you go. Whether it opens depends on the region, the town and the chain.

Regional chains

  • Consum: a Valencian cooperative that opened its first shop in Alaquàs (Valencia) in 1975. It has more than 1,000 supermarkets, counting its own Consum stores and its Charter franchise stores, in Catalonia, the Valencia region, Murcia, Castilla-La Mancha, Andalucía and Aragón. Charter stores are smaller shops for small towns and neighbourhoods. Official site: consum.es.
  • Other regional chains are strong in their own areas, for example Gadis in Galicia and Castilla y León, BonpreuEsclat in Catalonia and Supermercados MAS in Andalucía.

Spain also has many local markets, independent shops and smaller chains. A store finder or a map app is the quickest way to see what is near you.

Opening hours, Sundays and holidays

  • Regions set the rules. A national law (Law 1/2004 on shop opening hours) sets the framework and each region decides the details. From Monday to Saturday, a region cannot limit shops to fewer than 90 opening hours a week in total.
  • Sundays and public holidays: each region chooses the Sundays and holidays on which shops may open, at least 10 a year. On the other Sundays and holidays, larger supermarkets usually stay closed unless they are in a high-tourism zone.
  • Exceptions: small shops under 300 m² (unless they belong to a large group), bakeries, convenience stores, shops in stations and shops in areas declared high-tourism zones (zonas de gran afluencia turística, ZGAT) may choose their own days and hours.
  • Valencia region in 2026: larger shops may open on 11 authorised Sundays and holidays, and every shop must show a sign with its opening days and hours.

Opening hours vary from store to store. The store page on each chain’s website shows the hours, including special hours around public holidays.

Paying, bags and fresh counters

  • Paying: supermarket checkouts take bank cards as well as cash.
  • Bags: since 1 July 2018, shops in Spain may not give plastic bags to customers for free (Royal Decree 293/2018). Since 2021, light plastic bags are only allowed if they are compostable. Bring a reusable bag, or buy one at the checkout. The rule also covers online orders and home delivery.
  • Fresh counters: many larger supermarkets have staffed counters for meat (carnicería), cold meats and cheese (charcutería) and fish (pescadería), where customers are served in turn. At Consum, for example, the Mundo Consum app lets you take a turn on your phone for these three counters in stores that have the system.
  • Loyalty clubs: several chains have free customer clubs, such as Mundo Consum, El Club Carrefour and Club Alcampo. Check the conditions on each chain’s site.

Buying alcohol

  • Under 18s: in the Valencia region, selling alcohol to anyone under 18 is banned, and staff must ask for official ID if they are unsure of your age.
  • Late at night: in the Valencia region, shops may not sell alcohol between 22:00 and 07:00. This also covers home deliveries made during those hours. Bars and restaurants serving drinks on the premises are not affected.
  • Other regions have their own rules on alcohol sales. Signs in the shop and staff at the checkout will tell you.

Online shopping and home delivery

  • Mercadona, Carrefour, Dia, Eroski, Alcampo and Consum all run online grocery shops. Eroski, for example, offers home delivery and collection from a store.
  • Delivery areas and time slots depend on your address. Check your postcode in the online shop.
  • Lidl’s online shop mainly sells its non-food offers, such as home, DIY, sport and clothing items.

Alicante, Torrevieja and the Costa Blanca

  • Consum: as a Valencian chain, Consum has several supermarkets in Torrevieja. Its store pages list the address, opening hours, holiday hours and services such as home delivery.
  • Torrevieja is a high-tourism zone: the whole town is declared a ZGAT all year round, so shops there are free to choose their opening days and hours, except on 1 January, 6 January, 1 May, 9 October and 25 December.
  • Each shop still decides. Being allowed to open on a Sunday does not mean every supermarket does. Some stay closed on Sundays, so check the store page first.
  • Alcohol: the Valencia region rules above apply in Alicante and Torrevieja, including no shop sales between 22:00 and 07:00.

Supermarkets in Spain: quick answers

What are the main supermarket chains in Spain?

The main national chains are Mercadona, Carrefour, Lidl, Dia, Aldi, Eroski and Alcampo. Regional chains such as Consum, Gadis, BonpreuEsclat and Supermercados MAS are strong in their own areas.

Are supermarkets in Spain open on Sundays?

Usually not, apart from a set number of authorised Sundays and holidays each year chosen by each region. In towns declared high-tourism zones, such as Torrevieja, shops may open on Sundays, but each store decides.

Do Spanish supermarkets charge for plastic bags?

Yes. Shops may not give plastic bags away for free. Bring a reusable bag or buy one at the checkout.

Can I pay by card in Spanish supermarkets?

Yes. Checkouts take bank cards as well as cash.

Can I order groceries online in Spain?

Yes. Mercadona, Carrefour, Dia, Eroski, Alcampo and Consum have online shops. Delivery depends on your postcode.

Is Consum only in the Valencia region?

No. Consum is a Valencian cooperative, but it has supermarkets in Catalonia, the Valencia region, Murcia, Castilla-La Mancha, Andalucía and Aragón, including several in Torrevieja.

Can I buy alcohol in a supermarket late at night?

Not in the Valencia region, which includes Alicante and Torrevieja. Shops there may not sell alcohol between 22:00 and 07:00. Other regions have their own rules.

Documents that often slow people down

Identity and civil records

  • Valid passport or EU national ID.
  • Birth, marriage, or partnership records where relevant.
  • Legalization or Hague apostille for many foreign public documents.
  • Sworn Spanish translations when documents are not accepted as-is.

Spain-side basics

  • Address in Spain and, when requested, padrón certificate.
  • Immigration appointment confirmation.
  • Correct EX form for your procedure.
  • Paid tasa receipt, often Modelo 790 with the matching code.

Money, health, purpose

  • Proof of economic means for routes that require it.
  • Health insurance or public coverage proof where required.
  • School admission, remote-work evidence, family link, or permit approval.
  • Criminal record certificate for some visas and residence routes.

Not every document applies to every route. The guide should help you narrow this down.

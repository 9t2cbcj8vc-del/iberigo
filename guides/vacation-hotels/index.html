How hotels work in Spain

Star ratings in Spain are mainly a checklist of facilities and services: things like a lift, 24-hour reception, minimum room size, and other equipment. They do not tell you how recently the hotel was renovated, or how well it is kept. A newly refurbished 3-star boutique hotel or hostal can feel nicer than a dated 4-star. At the top end, some hotels are classed as 5-star Gran Lujo, a step above a standard 5-star.

Local stay types are easy to mix up, especially hostal and “hostel”:

  • Hotel: a standard hotel, from simple to luxury, with the usual reception and room service level for its star rating.
  • Hostal or pensión: simple private rooms, often en suite, often central and family-run. This is not a dorm hostel.
  • Albergue: a hostel with shared dorms, the kind of stay many travellers mean by “hostel”.
  • Aparthotel: apartment-style rooms or studios with a kitchenette, plus some hotel services.
  • Casa rural: countryside lodging, often in a village or a converted house, usually quieter and more informal than a city hotel.
  • Parador: a hotel in the state-run Paradores network, usually in a historic building such as a castle, monastery, palace or other landmark.

The lists below group well-known brands by the kind of stay they are known for. Quality can still vary from one hotel to the next, so always check the individual property.

Historic and character stays

  • Paradores

    Spain’s state-run network of hotels in historic buildings: castles, monasteries, palaces and other landmarks, in cities and in quieter spots. The buildings are the point of the stay, and the atmosphere is more heritage than trendy design.

    paradores.es

  • Rusticae

    A club of independently run charm hotels and rural houses, not a uniform chain. Expect characterful, smaller stays rather than a standardised hotel product. Rusticae is now part of the Barceló group, but the properties still feel individual.

    rusticae.es

  • Áurea (Eurostars)

    Eurostars’ signature collection in historic or landmark buildings. These hotels have more character and a stronger sense of place than a typical Eurostars city hotel.

    eurostarshotels.com

Design and lifestyle city breaks

  • ME by Meliá, INNSiDE by Meliá and Gran Meliá

    Three Meliá brands at the design and upscale end. ME is bold, social and lifestyle-led, often in lively city districts. INNSiDE is contemporary and informal, aimed at curious city stays. Gran Meliá is the group’s luxury collection, with a more polished, classic-upscale feel.

    ME by Meliá · INNSiDE · Gran Meliá

  • NH Collection and nhow

    NH’s higher and more design-led city brands. NH Collection is an upscale urban stay with stronger design and service than a standard NH hotel. nhow is the artistic, statement-design brand, with a more playful, creative atmosphere.

    NH Collection · nhow

  • Room Mate

    Playful, design-led city hotels, usually in central locations. The tone is informal and friendly rather than corporate, which suits a short city break.

    room-matehotels.com

  • H10 and The One

    H10 is a Spanish group with solid city and resort hotels. The One is its luxury urban collection: more refined design and a quieter, upscale feel than a standard H10.

    H10 Hotels · The One

  • Only YOU

    Stylish lifestyle hotels in central city locations, with a strong emphasis on design, social spaces and a slightly boutique atmosphere.

    onlyyouhotels.com

Beach resorts and sun holidays

  • Iberostar

    Polished beachfront resorts, well known for food and a clear sustainability push. Check the tier: family resorts sit alongside adults-only stays such as Iberostar Selection and JOIA, which feel quieter and more grown-up.

    iberostar.com

  • RIU

    Classic large coastal and island resorts, with big pools and a strong all-inclusive focus. Riu Palace is the higher, more comfortable tier within the same group.

    riu.com

  • Barceló and Occidental

    A wide mix of coastal and island resorts from the same group. Barceló sits toward the more upscale end; Occidental is usually a more relaxed mid-range beach stay.

    Barceló · Occidental

  • Palladium, TRS and Bless

    An upbeat, lifestyle-led resort group, especially strong in Ibiza and on the Costa del Sol. Bless is the luxury lifestyle collection; TRS is adults-only. The mood is generally livelier than a quiet family beach hotel.

    Palladium · TRS · Bless

  • Meliá, Paradisus and Sol

    Another case where the sub-brand matters. Meliá and Paradisus sit at the upscale resort end; Sol is the classic, more informal family beach-holiday brand.

    Meliá · Paradisus · Sol

Reliable mid-range city stays

  • NH Hotels

    A reliable mid-range city brand, usually in practical, central or business locations. Comfortable and predictable, with less design emphasis than NH Collection or nhow.

    nh-hotels.com

  • Eurostars and Exe

    Two Hotusa brands for urban stays. Eurostars is the more polished city hotel; Exe is the simpler, more functional mid-range sister brand.

    Eurostars · Exe

  • Catalonia Hotels

    A dependable mid-range Spanish group, with a strong presence in Catalonia and in other Spanish cities, plus some beach hotels. A straightforward city-stay choice.

    cataloniahotels.com

  • AC Hotels by Marriott

    Modern, sleek urban hotels. The brand was founded in Spain and later joined Marriott, so you get a contemporary city stay with the backing of a large international group.

    AC Hotels

  • Ibis and B&B Hotels

    Simple, clean, predictable budget-to-mid city stays. Rooms are compact and the service is no-frills, which suits a short overnight or a practical city base.

    Ibis · B&B Hotels

Family and apartment-style stays

  • Ona Hotels & Apartments

    Hotels and aparthotels aimed at longer or more independent stays, with apartment-style rooms that work well for families who want a kitchen and a bit more space.

    onahotels.com

  • Grupotel

    A Balearic-focused group with hotels and apartments that suit families, including stays with space, pools and a relaxed island pace.

    grupotel.com

  • Zafiro

    Another strong Balearic option, with hotels and apartments popular with families and with guests who want cycling or wellness facilities alongside a beach-holiday base.

    zafirohotels.com

Practical picking and booking tips

  • Check the sub-brand, not just the parent group. Meliá, NH, Barceló, Eurostars and others run several brands under one name. A Sol hotel is a very different stay from a Gran Meliá, just as Ibis is a different stay from Sofitel, even when the parent company is the same.
  • Look at renovation age and recent guest photos as well as the star rating. Stars measure facilities, not whether the rooms still feel fresh.
  • Compare the official site with a booking platform. Neither is automatically cheaper. Check cancellation rules, whether breakfast is included, and any member or loyalty perks before you pay. Our booking platforms guide covers the main sites.
  • Some regions charge a local tourist tax at check-in, including Catalonia and the Balearic Islands. The amount and the rules vary by place, and it is usually paid at the hotel rather than in the original online price.

Official brand websites are linked in each section above. Details can change, so confirm the property, the sub-brand and the booking conditions before you reserve.

Hotels in Spain: quick answers

What is the difference between a hostal and a hostel in Spain?

A hostal or pensión usually offers simple private rooms, often with an en-suite bathroom, and is frequently family-run and central. An albergue is a hostel with shared dorms. If you want a private room, look for a hostal, pensión or hotel, not an albergue.

Do Spanish star ratings tell you how recently a hotel was renovated?

No. Stars are mainly a facilities and services checklist, such as a lift, 24-hour reception or minimum room size. They do not measure how new the decoration is. Check recent photos and reviews as well as the star rating.

What are Paradores?

Paradores is a state-run network of hotels, usually in historic buildings such as castles, monasteries, palaces or other landmarks. They are a good choice if you want a stay with architectural character rather than a modern chain hotel.

Which hotel brands are better for a beach holiday, and which for a city break?

For a sun-and-beach holiday, look at resort brands such as Iberostar, RIU, Barceló, Occidental, Palladium, Sol or Meliá. For a city break, design and mid-range urban brands are usually a better fit: ME, INNSiDE, NH Collection, Room Mate, H10, Eurostars, Catalonia or AC Hotels. Sub-brands still matter within each group.

Is it better to book directly with the hotel or through a booking site?

Neither is always cheaper or more flexible. Compare the official site and a booking platform for the cancellation rules, breakfast, and any member rates or perks, then book the option that fits your plans.

Documents that often slow people down

Identity and civil records

  • Valid passport or EU national ID.
  • Birth, marriage, or partnership records where relevant.
  • Legalization or Hague apostille for many foreign public documents.
  • Sworn Spanish translations when documents are not accepted as-is.

Spain-side basics

  • Address in Spain and, when requested, padrón certificate.
  • Immigration appointment confirmation.
  • Correct EX form for your procedure.
  • Paid tasa receipt, often Modelo 790 with the matching code.

Money, health, purpose

  • Proof of economic means for routes that require it.
  • Health insurance or public coverage proof where required.
  • School admission, remote-work evidence, family link, or permit approval.
  • Criminal record certificate for some visas and residence routes.

Not every document applies to every route. The guide should help you narrow this down.

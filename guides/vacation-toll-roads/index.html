Toll Roads in Spain: A Simple Guide to AP and A Motorways (2026)

A simple introduction to toll roads in Spain: autopista (AP) vs autovía (A), which tolls have ended, how to pay with card, cash or VIA-T, and rental car tips. Spain has two main kinds of motorway. Autovías are numbered A- (for example A-7) and are free. Toll motorways are autopistas numbered AP- (for example AP-7). Many former toll sections are now free, including the whole AP-7 from the French border to Alicante, but they kept their AP number. Where tolls remain, you pay at a toll plaza by card, cash or an electronic VIA-T tag. Facts below were checked on official pages on 9 October 2026.

Autopista (AP) or autovía (A): how to tell them apart

  • Road numbers: on the state network, motorways and autovías are numbered with an “A” on a blue background. Toll sections add a “P”, so they read AP (for example AP-7). Madrid’s toll access roads keep the letter R (R-2 to R-5).
  • Signs: the start of an autopista and the start of an autovía each have their own blue sign (S-1 “Autopista” and S-1a “Autovía” in the official sign catalogue). Both mean motorway traffic rules apply from that point.
  • The legal difference: an autopista is built only for motor vehicles, with no access from roadside properties and no level crossings. An autovía also has separate carriageways for each direction, limited access from roadside properties and no level crossings, but does not meet every autopista requirement. Paying is not part of either definition.

An AP number does not always mean you pay today. Sections that stopped charging tolls since 2018 kept their AP numbers. The useful question is whether the section you will drive is still on the toll list.

Many tolls have ended

Since 2018 the Spanish government has let toll concessions expire instead of renewing them. The ministry says more than 1,000 km of state toll motorways became free this way.

Where tolls still apply

The ministry’s list of state toll motorways still charging includes:

  • Mediterranean coast: AP-7 Málaga–Estepona and Estepona–Guadiaro (Costa del Sol), AP-7 Alicante–Cartagena, and AP-7 Cartagena–Vera.
  • Around Madrid: the radial toll roads R-2, R-3, R-4 and R-5, the M-12, AP-41 Madrid–Toledo and AP-36 Ocaña–La Roda, plus AP-6 Villalba–Adanero and its branches AP-51 (Ávila) and AP-61 (Segovia).
  • North and northwest: AP-9 Ferrol–Portuguese border and AP-53 in Galicia, AP-66 Campomanes–León, AP-71 León–Astorga, and AP-68 Bilbao–Zaragoza.
  • Andalucía inland: AP-46 Alto de las Pedrizas–Málaga.

Regional governments run other toll roads that are not on this state list: in Catalonia, Navarra, Galicia and the Basque Country (Gipuzkoa and Bizkaia). Watch the signs there too.

The toll roads run by the state company SEITT (the Madrid radials, M-12, AP-36, AP-41 and AP-7 Cartagena–Vera) are free every day between 00:00 and 06:00.

How paying works

  • Card or cash: toll plazas have lanes where you pay by card or cash. If you have no tag, use these lanes.
  • Ticket roads: on some sections you take a ticket when you enter and pay when you leave. Use the same way of paying at both ends: if you entered through a VIA-T lane, leave through a VIA-T lane too.
  • VIA-T tag: VIA-T is the electronic toll system used on all Spanish toll motorways. A small device on the windscreen is read by an antenna as you drive through slowly, and the toll is charged to the linked bank account or card. One beep means it worked and the barrier opens. No beep, or several beeps, means it failed and the barrier stays down, so you must pay another way.
  • Lane signs: a telepeaje sign (S-32) marks lanes where you can pay with a tag. A “telepeaje obligatorio” sign (R-418) marks tag-only lanes. Do not enter those without a working tag.

Banks and other companies listed on the VIA-T website issue tags, linked to an account or card. VIA-T also works on Portuguese toll roads and some French motorways; ask the issuer first. In Portugal, the electronic lanes are Via Verde lanes, marked with a V on a green background. Some Spanish toll roads charge less when you pay by tag.

Rental cars

  • You pay the tolls. Rental conditions make the driver responsible for tolls during the rental.
  • Ask at pickup whether the car has a toll tag and how tolls are billed. Not every company offers one. Avis Spain, for example, says it does not offer electronic toll devices.
  • Charges can come later. If a toll or fine reaches the rental company after you return the car, it can charge you then, plus an admin fee on top.
  • One tag only. If you bring your own VIA-T, do not carry it in a car that already has a tag. The VIA-T website warns that both can be charged, and that a tag can be read even when you do not mean to use it.

Alicante, Torrevieja and the Costa Blanca

  • North of Alicante: the AP-7 towards Valencia and Tarragona has been toll-free since 1 January 2020.
  • Alicante bypass: the AP-7 Circunvalación de Alicante is now permanently toll-free.
  • South of Alicante: the AP-7 Alicante–Cartagena is still a toll road. It starts at a junction with the free A-7 autovía and has exits for the Torrevieja area, including CV-90 Torrevieja, San Miguel de Salinas–Torrevieja, Los Montesinos, La Zenia, Campoamor and Pilar de la Horadada. Its main toll barriers are at Los Montesinos and La Zenia. The ministry publishes higher high-season rates for June to September and around Easter.
  • Further south: the AP-7 Cartagena–Vera (Murcia to Almería) is also tolled, and is free at night from 00:00 to 06:00.

Toll roads in Spain: quick answers

Are all motorways in Spain toll roads?

No. Autovías, numbered A-, are free. Toll motorways are numbered AP-, and many AP sections no longer charge a toll.

Is the AP-7 still a toll road?

Mostly not. The AP-7 from the French border to Alicante has been free since 2020 and 2021, and the Alicante bypass is free. Tolls remain on AP-7 Alicante–Cartagena, Cartagena–Vera, and Málaga–Estepona–Guadiaro.

Can I pay Spanish tolls by card?

Yes. Toll plazas have lanes for card or cash, plus VIA-T lanes for cars with a tag. Without a tag, use a card or cash lane.

What is VIA-T?

Spain’s electronic toll tag. It is a small device on the windscreen that pays the toll as you drive through a VIA-T lane. Banks and other issuers provide it.

Do rental cars come with a toll tag?

It depends on the company, so ask at pickup. Either way, you pay the tolls, and charges can arrive after the rental.

Is the road to Torrevieja a toll road?

The AP-7 Alicante–Cartagena, which has exits for the Torrevieja area, is tolled. The AP-7 north of Alicante and the Alicante bypass are free. The free A-7 autovía meets the toll section where it starts.

Documents that often slow people down

Identity and civil records

  • Valid passport or EU national ID.
  • Birth, marriage, or partnership records where relevant.
  • Legalization or Hague apostille for many foreign public documents.
  • Sworn Spanish translations when documents are not accepted as-is.

Spain-side basics

  • Address in Spain and, when requested, padrón certificate.
  • Immigration appointment confirmation.
  • Correct EX form for your procedure.
  • Paid tasa receipt, often Modelo 790 with the matching code.

Money, health, purpose

  • Proof of economic means for routes that require it.
  • Health insurance or public coverage proof where required.
  • School admission, remote-work evidence, family link, or permit approval.
  • Criminal record certificate for some visas and residence routes.

Not every document applies to every route. The guide should help you narrow this down.

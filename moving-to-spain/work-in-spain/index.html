Moving to Spain for Work

A practical starting point for understanding work-related routes, what you may need to prepare, and which steps usually come before and after starting work in Spain.

7 min read

Quick Answer

People moving to Spain for work may follow different routes depending on nationality and job situation. EU citizens and non-EU citizens follow different processes. Non-EU citizens usually need the correct work and residence authorisation before working. Employees, highly qualified workers, seasonal workers and remote workers may follow different routes. This roadmap helps you understand the general journey — it does not replace official requirements.

EU citizens working in Spain

Usually no visa needed

EU, EEA and Swiss citizens do not usually need a visa to work in Spain.

EU registration if staying longer

If staying longer than three months, EU Registration may apply.

Connected to Social Security, healthcare and tax

Employment can affect Social Security, healthcare and tax obligations.

Keep your records

Workers should keep contracts, payslips and Social Security records.

Non-EU citizens working in Spain

Correct route needed first

Non-EU citizens usually need the correct work and residence route before working.

Different channels depending on route

TIE may be required after approval or arrival.

Identity

A valid passport or national ID is usually central to the process.

Job offer or employment contract

A job offer or employment contract is usually needed to support the route.

Qualification documents, if relevant

Some roles may require recognised qualification documents.

Professional recognition documents, if relevant

Some professions may require recognition of foreign qualifications.

Criminal record certificate, where required

Some routes may ask for a criminal record certificate.

Healthcare documents, where required

Some routes may ask for healthcare evidence.

Translations, legalisation or apostille, where required

Some foreign documents may need official translation, legalisation or apostille.

NIE/TIE/residence documents, depending on route

Identification and residence documents depend on your route and stage of the process.

Social Security registration, where applicable

Social Security registration may be part of starting work.

Employer role

Employer may need to start or support the process

In some work routes, the employer may need to start or support the process.

Social Security registration

Employees are usually registered with Social Security by the employer once the employment relationship is valid.

Ask for confirmation

Workers should ask for confirmation of registration and keep copies.

Still check official requirements

Employer involvement does not remove the need to check official requirements yourself.

Real Questions People Ask

Can I move to Spain for work?

Often yes, but the correct route depends on your nationality, job offer and situation. Check the official requirements that apply to your case.

Can EU citizens work in Spain without a visa?

Usually yes. EU, EEA and Swiss citizens do not usually need a visa to work, though EU Registration may apply if staying longer than three months.

Can non-EU citizens work in Spain with only a job offer?

Not by itself. A job offer usually needs to be supported by the correct work and residence authorisation before working legally.

Does my employer register me with Social Security?

Employers usually handle this once the employment relationship is valid, but you should ask for confirmation and keep your own records.
